On Improving Graph Neural Networks for QSAR by Pre-training on Extended-Connectivity Fingerprints

This paper investigates the influence of substructure-level data leakage during pre-training on Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) using Extended-Connectivity Fingerprints (ECFPs), a common approach in drug discovery, particularly for Quantitative Structure-Activity Relationship (QSAR) studies. The study reveals that while there are scenarios where ECFP-based pre-training may underperform, it can significantly improve out-of-distribution performance on various practical QSAR tasks, suggesting its potential value in enhancing the predictive capabilities of GNNs for drug discovery applications.

Bayesian Optimization & Active Learning

#2

Active Learning for Gaussian Process Regression Under Self-Induced Boltzmann Weights

This paper introduces AB-SID-iVAR, a novel Gaussian Process (GP) acquisition function for active learning. It approximates the Bayesian target distribution in closed form without requiring partition function estimation, applicable to both discrete and continuous input domains. The method shows consistent improvements over existing approaches on synthetic benchmarks and real-world PES modeling and drug discovery tasks, demonstrating its potential significance for drug discovery, cheminformatics, and molecular machine learning by addressing the active learning problem under an unknown Boltzmann distribution induced by the function itself.

ToolMol: Evolutionary Agentic Framework for Multi-objective Drug Discovery

This paper presents the evolutionary agentic framework, ToolMol, for multi-objective drug discovery. It demonstrates superior performance on property optimization tasks, discovering ligands with over 10% stronger predicted binding affinity compared to existing methods across three protein targets. Notably, ToolMol ligands also excel in Absolute Binding Free Energy scores, surpassing existing methods by more than 35%. This suggests ToolMol could potentially lead to the discovery of novel, high-performing drug candidates.

Molecular Representation Learning

#4

Physical probes expose and alleviate chemical-environment collapse in molecular representations

This paper introduces CLAIM (Contrastive Learning for Atom-to-molecule Inference of Molecular NMR), a framework that aligns topological molecular inputs with atom-resolved NMR observables to address the chemical-environment collapse issue in molecular representations. The method demonstrates robust performance in 13C NMR prediction, improves stereoisomer discrimination without explicit 3D modeling, and shows transferability to broader molecular property tasks such as ADMET prediction and fluorescence estimation. These findings establish physically grounded spectral alignment as an effective strategy for alleviating chemical-environment collapse and guiding experimentally grounded molecular representation learning.

#5

QT-Net: Rethinking Evaluation of AI Models in Atomic Chemical Space

This paper introduces QT-Net, a model for evaluating AI models within atomic chemical space. The evaluation protocol clusters atomic environments using SOAP descriptors and computes metrics based on unseen cluster labels during training. Notably, the computed molecular dipole moments from QT-Net's per-atom outputs align with ground-truth values reported in QM9. This suggests that QT-Net can infer properties of atoms in molecules outside its training set, potentially improving downstream molecular property prediction tasks.

QSAR & ADMET

#6

Architecture-Specific Failure Modes in hERG QSAR Predictions for Iboga Alkaloids: A Within-Family Empirical Test of the Binding-vs-Blockade Limitation

This paper investigates the limitations of Pred-hERG, a QSAR model for predicting hERG inhibition, using the iboga alkaloid compound family. The study reveals that despite low structural similarity among the eight tested compounds, Pred-hERG consistently labels seven as "Inside AD", while flagging only oxa-noribogaine, which has a benzofuran-replaced indole, as "Outside AD". This asymmetric failure pattern in Pred-hERG's applicability domain assessment provides a previously undescribed elaboration to the CiPA-aligned literature on clinically active compound classes.

#7

CADMA: Contribution-Aware Dynamic Multi-Modal Attention for Molecular Property Prediction and Chemical Reaction Analysis

This paper introduces CADMA, a Contribution-Aware Dynamic Multi-modal Attention framework for molecular property prediction and chemical reaction analysis. The model addresses limitations in current methods by introducing three key innovations, including adaptive modality contribution adjustment and domain knowledge integration. Extensive experiments on MoleculeNet, QM9, USPTO-50K benchmarks show that CADMA outperforms existing methods, achieving high AUC scores (up to 0.931 on BACE) and top-1 accuracy (53.1% on USPTO-50K reaction prediction). The model's superior performance and interpretability make it a promising tool for drug discovery and cheminformatics research.

Uncertainty Quantification

#8

LiTENexus: An End-to-End Virtual Drug Discovery System Based on Quantum Chemical Grounding

The LiTENexus system offers a revolutionary end-to-end virtual drug discovery approach. It combines quantum chemical grounding with physics-informed neural operators, achieving quantum-level accuracy in microscopic property derivation, up to 2-3 orders of magnitude faster than standard Density Functional Theory (DFT). In macroscopic pharmacokinetic prediction, it outperforms over 20 baselines on Biogen dataset and excels in 25 of 33 PharmaBench metrics, dominating 62 of 77 ADMETLAB 3.0 tasks.

#9

Enabling Structure-Only Initialization and Out-of-Distribution Generalization in GNN-based Molecular Dynamics Simulators

This paper presents a novel approach to molecular dynamics simulations using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). The study introduces a differentiable GNN-based barostat for accurate tracking of system dimensions and pressure, crucial for capturing macroscopic responses and supporting out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. By implementing these methods, the research significantly improves rollout stability and enables reliable OOD generalization, even in regimes with complex dynamics distinct from those in the training data. This work could potentially revolutionize the efficiency of modeling complex systems for materials design.
