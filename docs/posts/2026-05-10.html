Bayesian Optimization & Active Learning

#2

Pretrained Model Representations as Acquisition Signals for Active Learning of MLIPs

This paper presents two acquisition signals derived from a pretrained MACE potential: a finite-width neural tangent kernel (NTK) and an activation kernel built from hidden latent space features. The pretrained model induces similarity spaces that preserve chemically meaningful structure, providing more reliable residual uncertainty estimates than other kernels. On reactive-chemistry benchmarks, both kernels outperform fixed-descriptor baselines, committee disagreement, and random acquisition, reducing the data required to reach performance targets by an average of 38% for energy error and 28% for force error, demonstrating their potential in active learning of MLIPs.

#3

Regime-Conditioned Evaluation in Multi-Context Bayesian Optimization

In the multi-context Bayesian optimization, a hierarchical model reveals that the performance difference between Greedy and UCB strategies can reverse depending on the budget (B=50 vs B=100) in the GDSC2 benchmark, with a maximum gap of 0.050 Hit@1. Across 79 conditions, a significant fraction (19%) shows minimal difference (<0.01 Hit@1) between these strategies. Pre-registered predictions demonstrate an overall accuracy of 67.5%, and high accuracy within EMA prior families exceeding 90%.

#4

Discovering Reaction Mechanisms with Transition Path Sampling-Based Active Learning of Machine-Learned Potentials

This paper presents an active learning framework that utilizes Transition Path Sampling (TPS) to generate targeted data for Machine Learning Potential (MLP) models, improving their accuracy in barrier regions. Applied to the electrochemical CO$_2$ reduction to CO on copper in explicit water, this approach eliminates nonphysical artifacts present in early models and achieves near-DFT energy and force accuracy. The resulting MLPs enable stable long-time sampling of reactive pathways, addressing a common challenge of MLP reliability in transition-state regions governing rare events. This work could potentially streamline the computational analysis of complex chemical reactions.

Computational Chemistry

#5

CARD: Coarse-to-fine Autoregressive Modeling with Radix-based Decomposition for Transferable Free Energy Estimation

This paper introduces CARD, a generative model that utilizes radix-based decomposition for coarse-to-fine autoregressive modeling of 3D molecular coordinates. The novel approach aims to enhance expressiveness and address the limitations of existing methods, which are either prohibitively expensive due to reliance on extensive molecular dynamics simulations or constrained by less-expressive generative models or input dimensions tied to a specific system. Experimental results across various tasks show that CARD matches the accuracy of classical computational methods on unseen systems with diverse topologies, while achieving an approximately 40-fold speedup in inference. This could potentially revolutionize drug discovery and cheminformatics by offering a more efficient and versatile free energy estimation method.

Drug Discovery & Cheminformatics

#6

Benchmarking open-source tools for in silico antiviral drug discovery

This paper studies the performance of various open-source computational tools for in silico antiviral drug discovery, with a focus on AI-based systems and docking tools. The study found that Boltz-2 and DrugFormDTA performed best among machine learning approaches, while GNINA excelled among docking approaches, although performance varied across specific viral proteins. The research further benchmarked 15 open-source binding affinity prediction tools using a custom test set of 853 antiviral compounds across 16 protein targets from 10 virus species. This work provides valuable insights for selecting effective tools in the ongoing pursuit of antiviral drug discovery.

#7

SPADE: Faster Drug Discovery by Learning from Sparse Data

This paper presents SPADE, a novel algorithm for drug discovery that excels in sparse data scenarios. On average, SPADE requires just 40 tests to identify 10 high-quality ligands. In one-vs-one comparisons, SPADE demonstrates superior sample efficiency over deep learning and Bayesian optimization methods, achieving median improvements of 7%-32%. The significance of this work lies in its potential to accelerate drug discovery processes by reducing the number of required tests.

Molecular Representation Learning

#8

Molecules Meet Language: Confound-Aware Representation Learning and Chemical Property Steering in Transformer-VAE Latent Spaces

This paper presents a method called Confound-Aware Representation Learning within Transformer-VAE latent spaces, aimed at separating chemical signals from artifacts in the SELFIES dataset. The approach demonstrates that apparent property predictability in molecular generative models can be due to sequence-level shortcuts rather than chemical organization. The main result shows that chemically meaningful steering can emerge in entangled molecular latent spaces, but only when validated through decoded molecules and controlled for representation-level confounds. This work highlights the importance of understanding and addressing confounding factors in molecular representation learning to ensure accurate property predictions.
