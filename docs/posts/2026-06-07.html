Generative Chemistry & Molecular Design Highest ranked paper this week

Genotype-Conditioned Molecular Generation via Evidence-Grounded Multi-Objective Latent Perturbation in Diffusion Models

This paper introduces a latent-space optimization approach for a pretrained genotype-to-drug diffusion model, utilizing a learnable perturbation to optimize a composite reward that considers predicted drug sensitivity (AUC), drug-likeness (QED), and synthetic accessibility (SAS). The method also incorporates a multi-agent language model pipeline grounded in the diffusion model's attention mechanism for mechanistic consistency. Experiments on 15 cancer cell lines from three held-out evaluation sets show consistent and significant improvements over competing baselines in terms of sensitivity, drug-likeness, synthesizability, and chemical validity. This work could potentially advance drug discovery by providing a more comprehensive and plausible molecular generation approach.

Bayesian Optimization & Active Learning

#2

Evidence-Gated LLM Priors for Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization

This paper explores the utilization of Large Language Model (LLM) generated expert priors in multi-objective Bayesian optimization, addressing the issue of uncalibrated suggestions from these models. The study presents a method to dynamically calibrate LLM priors based on downstream objective values, improving robustness over fixed LLM priors. This approach was tested across synthetic stress tests and three molecule optimization benchmarks using \qwenflash{}-generated expert priors.

#3

Towards Automated Discovery: A Review of Generative Models, Multimodal Learning and Closed-Loop Workflows in Inverse Materials Design

This paper reviews advancements in generative crystal structure modeling, multimodal learning, and closed-loop design pipelines for crystalline solids within the context of inverse materials design. The approach aims to transition materials discovery from predictive models to targeted proposals that meet physical constraints. Notably, the review highlights the potential of multimodal learning to integrate various materials modalities, such as crystal structures, thermodynamic, electronic information, microscopy, spectroscopy, processing context, and scientific text, creating a more universal and transferable representation of chemical space.

Computational Chemistry

#4

Physics-Based vs AI-Based Free Energy Prediction for Protein-Ligand Potency: Public Benchmarks and Internal Project Evidence

This paper investigates the factors influencing the accuracy and robustness of modern Reaction-Based Free Energy (RBFE) workflows for predicting protein-ligand potency. It compares the performance of physics-based and AI methods, finding that while AI methods excel on curated benchmarks, physics-based free energy calculations are crucial for decision-making in unseen chemical matter. The study also highlights improvements through localized force field refinements, protein conformational control, and alchemical schedule design.

#5

Using Relative Binding Free Energy Calculations for Rank Ordering of Endocrine Disrupting Compounds to the Human Estrogen Receptor Alpha

This paper explores the application of alchemical relative binding free energy (RBFE) calculations for ranking endocrine disrupting compounds (EDCs) towards the human Estrogen Receptor Alpha (ERα). The study demonstrates that molecular docking, particularly with receptor flexibility taken into account, can accurately predict EDC binding poses to ERα, potentially enhancing computational predictions in risk assessment for EDCs.

Generative Chemistry & Molecular Design

#6

Synthesis-Aware Molecular Generation via Reaction-Driven Global Search

This paper introduces RxnMol, a framework for synthesis-aware molecular optimization. It encodes candidates as synthetic-route representations of readily available building blocks, which are converted into products through sequential reaction prediction and population-based global optimization. In protein target and bioactivity optimization tasks, RxnMol demonstrates improved Top-1 performance compared to generative baselines and synthesis-constrained methods in most benchmarks, suggesting its potential for efficient drug discovery and cheminformatics applications.

#7

Fairness Definitions and Metrics in Deep Reinforcement Learning for Drug Discovery in Healthcare: A Rapid Evidence Review

This paper focuses on fairness definitions and metrics in Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for drug discovery, specifically in molecule generation within healthcare. The study presents a concise set of fairness criteria, including distributional balance in physicochemical descriptors, scaffold/chemotype diversity, groupwise validity, toxicity, and synthetic accessibility as key metrics. These findings offer valuable guidance for ensuring equitable outcomes in DRL-driven drug discovery processes.

#8

VALIDITY IS NOT ENOUGH: QUANTIFYING STRUCTURAL COLLAPSE AND INHERITANCE LOSS IN SELFIES-BASED MOLECULAR CROSSOVER

This paper investigates the impact of SELFIES correction on molecular crossover in genetic algorithms (GAs), using drug-like molecules from the ZINC20 database. Through 10,000 large-scale simulations and multi-generational experiments, it reveals that while SELFIES ensures chemically valid generations, its correction mechanism may unintentionally alter crossover semantics and limit structural diversity during evolutionary exploration. This finding highlights the need for a better understanding of SELFIES' effects on molecular evolution in drug discovery and cheminformatics.

#9

Generative Glide: A De Novo Workflow for Ultra-large Library Screening

The Generative Glide workflow, an integrated framework linking in silico generation to physical acquisition, utilizes active learning to enhance its predictive accuracy within high-potency domains. This refinement allows for effective navigation and exploitation of underrepresented low-scoring compounds in the initial training data. Deployed across various protein targets and multiple ultra-large libraries, it consistently achieves a significant improvement in docking score distributions towards better scoring compounds while maintaining scaffold diversity.

QSAR & ADMET

#10

ChemDistill: Hierarchical Graph Attention Distillation with Parameter-Efficient Multi-Task Adapters for Molecular Property Prediction and Chemical Reaction Analysis

This paper introduces ChemDistill, a framework for chemical knowledge distillation utilizing hierarchical graph attention and parameter-efficient multi-task adapters. The proposed method aims to address challenges in molecular property prediction and chemical reaction analysis, particularly cross-domain knowledge transfer, multi-task learning for diverse endpoints, and the integration of spectral data with molecular graph representations. On the Tox21 benchmark, ChemDistill achieves a ROCAUC of 0.879, outperforming the teacher model ChemBERTa while being significantly more compact, demonstrating its potential for efficient and effective drug discovery and cheminformatics applications.
