Structure-guided molecular design with contrastive 3D protein-ligand learning

This paper introduces a unified framework for structure-guided molecular design, combining contrastive 3D protein-ligand learning and autoregressive molecular generation. The SE(3)-equivariant transformer encodes ligand and pocket structures into a shared embedding space, demonstrating competitive results in zero-shot virtual screening. The model generates target-specific molecules conditioned on either pocket or ligand structures, steering the output towards desired chemical spaces, resulting in candidates with favorable predicted binding properties across diverse targets. This approach could potentially streamline drug discovery processes by enabling the generation of tailored compounds for specific protein pockets.

Drug Discovery & Cheminformatics

#2

Discovery of novel and highly potent XIAP-targeted peptide inhibitors using virtual screening.

This paper explores the discovery of potent peptide inhibitors targeting XIAP, focusing on virtual screening methodology. The standout candidate, Peptide-5, demonstrates exceptional binding affinity (Kd = 10.2 ± 0.5 pM) to the BIR3 domain of XIAP, competing effectively with caspase-9. Furthermore, Peptide-5 exhibits promising serum and metabolic stability, along with favourable cellular permeability, making it a noteworthy candidate for further investigation in drug discovery and cheminformatics.

#3

Next-generation Janus kinase inhibitors: Integrating synthetic innovation, structural biology, and computational design for precision drug discovery.

This review integrates synthetic innovation, structural biology, and computational design to advance next-generation Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors for oncology, immunology, and chronic inflammatory diseases. Key strategies include hinge-binding optimization, heterocyclic diversification, multicomponent reactions, scaffold-hopping, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, QM/MM calculations, and machine-learning-based predictive modeling. These multidisciplinary approaches have accelerated hit discovery, refined selectivity, and improved the pharmacokinetic and safety profiles of emerging JAK inhibitors, offering promising prospects for precision drug discovery.

#4

Predictive Modeling of Natural Medicinal Compounds for Alzheimer Disease Using Cheminformatics

This paper introduces a cheminformatics model for predicting natural medicinal compounds effective against Alzheimer's disease. The approach combines molecular descriptors and machine learning to screen potential anti-Alzheimer drugs. A Random Forest classifier, trained on approved treatments, demonstrates moderate performance, achieving precision of 0.5970 and recall of 0.6590, identifying 73 candidate compounds. This work could facilitate the discovery of novel therapeutic options for Alzheimer's disease.

#5

Drug-likeness prioritised selection identifies anti-Escherichia coli candidates confirmed by molecular docking, dynamics simulations, and antibacterial assays.

This paper presents a comprehensive screening strategy for identifying anti-Escherichia coli candidates, focusing on maximizing drug-likeness. The strategy involves 33 predictive submodels across five dimensions: physicochemical properties, pharmacokinetics, safety, efficacy, and stability. Notably, the candidate compound M3 demonstrated superior binding stability compared to the reference drug Cefaclor, as confirmed by molecular docking, dynamics simulations, and antibacterial assays. This strategy could potentially aid in balancing antibacterial potency and developability in cephalosporin discovery against Escherichia coli.

#6

Integrating virtual screening and molecular dynamics simulations to identify emodin as a PYCR1 inhibitor modulating docetaxel sensitivity in prostate cancer.

This paper integrates virtual screening and molecular dynamics simulations to identify emodin, an anthraquinone compound, as a potential PYCR1 inhibitor modulating docetaxel (DTX) sensitivity in prostate cancer. Through structure-based virtual screening, molecular dynamics simulations, and cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA), emodin was identified as a PYCR1-targeting agent. The findings suggest that PYCR1 may be a key target mediating DTX resistance in prostate cancer, and the emodin-DTX combination could offer a promising potential clinical strategy to overcome such resistance.

Generative Chemistry & Molecular Design

#7

How Creative Are Large Language Models in Generating Molecules?

This paper investigates the creative capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) in generating molecular structures. The study conducts a comprehensive empirical evaluation across physicochemical, ADMET, and biological activity tasks. The results suggest that LLMs display unique patterns of creative behavior in molecule generation, notably an increase in constraint satisfaction when additional constraints are imposed. This research offers insights into the potential and limitations of using LLMs for molecular design and synthesis.
